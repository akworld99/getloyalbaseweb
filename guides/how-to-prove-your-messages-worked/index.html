How to tell if your messages really brought customers back

"She ordered after my message" isn't proof. Some customers would have ordered anyway. Here's the simple way to know the difference.

Updated 7 November 20262 min readBy the Loyalbase team
Short answer

Keep a random 1 in 10 of the people you planned to message aside, and don't message them. After 60 days, compare how many in each group ordered. The gap between the two groups is what your messages added. The rest would have ordered anyway.

Why "they ordered after my message" isn't proof

If you message 90 customers and 26 order, it's tempting to credit the message with 26 orders. But some of them were going to order that week anyway. Without a comparison, you can't tell how many.

What a held back group is

Before you send, set aside a random few people from the same list and don't message them. Same type of customer, same timing, one difference: no message. Whatever they do is what would have happened anyway.

A worked example

100 customers are on your late regulars list. You message 90 and hold back 10. After 60 days:

MessagedHeld back
People9010
Ordered within 60 days261
Came back rate29%10%

Without a message, about 10% would have ordered anyway. That's about 9 of the 90 you messaged. So your messages added about 17 extra orders (26 minus 9). If your average order is $60, that's about $1,020 in sales you wouldn't have had.

How Loyalbase counts a customer who came back

  • A message counts when the customer pays for an order within 60 days of it.
  • Each order is credited once, to the earliest message before it, so messaging someone three times never counts as three wins.
  • Messages less than 14 days old are left out of the came back rate, because those customers haven't had time to order yet.
  • 1 in 10 people on the yearly occasion, late regular, first time buyer and stopped ordering lists are held back at random. You can switch this off in settings.

Rules for a fair test

  1. Choose who to hold back at random. Not your favourite customers, not the hardest cases.
  2. Hold back from the same list. Compare late regulars with late regulars.
  3. Don't contact the held back group another way. A promo email to them spoils the comparison.
  4. Read results over months, not days. Small groups swing a lot.

Questions

Don't I lose sales by holding people back?

Possibly a few orders from 1 in 10 people. In exchange, you learn which messages are worth sending. In Loyalbase you can switch it off at any time.

How many customers do I need for this to work?

Small lists swing a lot. With 10 people held back, one order moves their rate by 10 points. Read the results over two or three months.

Is this the same as an A/B test?

It's close. An A/B test compares two versions of a message. A held back group compares sending a message with sending nothing.
