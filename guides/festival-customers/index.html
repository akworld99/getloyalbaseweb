How to find customers who order for the same festival every year

Your Eid regulars, Christmas regulars and Avurudu regulars are already in your order history. Here's how to find them, and when to reach them.

Updated 7 November 20265 min readBy the Loyalbase team
Short answer

Pick a short ordering window for each festival, for example the 10 days before Eid ul-Fitr. Tag every order that falls in that window, year by year, and leave out days that fall in two festivals' windows. Customers with orders in the window in two or more different years are your festival regulars. Message them about three weeks before the festival, before they start shopping around. Loyalbase does this automatically for Christmas, Avurudu, Eid ul-Fitr, Eid ul-Adha, Vesak, Deepavali and Thai Pongal.

Why festival regulars are worth finding

A festival buyer isn't a random shopper. Someone who bought from you before Eid two years running has a habit, and a reason that comes back every year. They're the easiest people to sell to next Eid, if you reach them before they start browsing.

The trouble is that festival orders hide inside normal sales. A customer who orders twice a year looks quiet most of the time. Unless you look by festival, you won't see the pattern.

Step 1: choose a window for each festival

People don't order on the day itself. They order in the days before. A window that's too wide catches ordinary orders. One that's too narrow misses early planners. These are the windows Loyalbase uses:

FestivalOrders that countWhen to message
Christmas15 days before, up to the day4 weeks before
Avurudu13 days before to 1 day after3 weeks before

Eid ul-Adha (Hajj)7 days before to 1 day after2 weeks before
Vesak7 days before to 1 day after2 weeks before
Deepavali10 days before, up to the day3 weeks before
Thai Pongal6 days before to 1 day after10 days before

Eid, Vesak and Deepavali follow moon calendars and move every year, sometimes by a day either way. Use the dates announced for your country.

Step 2: tag each order, year by year

For each year, mark every order inside a festival's window with that festival. Some years, two festivals sit close together. Avurudu and Eid ul-Fitr can land in the same weeks. When a day falls inside two windows, don't count it for either. That way an order is never put down to the wrong festival.

Step 3: find people with two or more years

Count, for each customer and festival, how many different years they ordered in that window. One year might be a coincidence. Two or more is a habit. That's your list.

Doing it in a spreadsheet

  1. Export your orders with the order date and the customer's phone or email.
  2. Add a column for each festival. Mark it with the year when the order date is inside that year's window.
  3. Clear the mark when the date is inside two festivals' windows.
  4. Make a pivot table: customers down the side, festivals across the top, count of different years in the cells.
  5. Filter for 2 or more.

It works, but plan an afternoon, and redo it before every festival.

What to send, and when

Message festival regulars before the window opens, so you're the first shop they think of. About three weeks before is right for most festivals, four for Christmas, and longer for companies ordering gifts.

Festival regular

The customer who orders every festival

When to send: about three weeks before the festival, to customers who ordered for it in at least two years.

Hi [Name]! You've ordered from us for [festival] the last two years, thank you. [Festival] is on [date] this year. Want us to get your order ready early? Orders before [cut-off] are guaranteed in time. [Shop name]

Why it works: It thanks them for a habit they may not have noticed, and gives a practical reason to order now.

Lapsed festival buyer

Last year's festival buyer who never came back

When to send: three weeks before the festival, to people who ordered before it last year and haven't ordered since.

Hi [Name], last [festival] you ordered from us. It's coming up again on [date]. Shall we help this year too? Here's what's new: [link] [Shop name]

Why it works: A light reminder of what they did last year, with something new to look at.

Questions

Which festivals does Loyalbase group customers by?

Christmas, Avurudu, Eid ul-Fitr, Eid ul-Adha, Vesak, Deepavali and Thai Pongal. Ask also understands Valentine's Day, Women's Day, Mother's Day, Father's Day, Children's Day, Ramadan, 11.11, Black Friday and New Year's Eve as date ranges.

What if a customer orders in two festivals that overlap?

Days that fall inside two festivals' windows in the same year count for neither, so an order is never counted for the wrong festival.

Is Ramadan a festival group?

Ramadan is understood as a date range in Ask, so you can see who ordered during Ramadan or compare Ramadan sales year on year. The festival groups use Eid ul-Fitr.

How many years of orders do I need?

Two festivals' worth to find regulars, one to find last year's buyers. Loyalbase reads your full order history when you connect, so most stores have several years on day one.
