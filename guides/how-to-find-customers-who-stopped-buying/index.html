How to find customers who stopped buying from your store

One fixed rule like "no order in 90 days" gets it wrong for most customers. Here's the method that works, by hand or automatically.

Updated 7 November 20263 min readBy the Loyalbase team
Short answer

Work out each customer's usual time between orders, then flag anyone who has gone clearly longer than that. Someone who orders every three weeks and hasn't ordered in six is slipping. Someone who orders twice a year isn't. Also check first time buyers 45 to 150 days after their first order, and anyone who orders around the same date every year.

Why one cut off date doesn't work

A coffee customer who orders every three weeks is long gone after 90 days. A customer who buys a gift twice a year is perfectly normal at 90 days. One number for everyone flags the wrong people and misses the right ones.

The fix is to judge each customer against their own rhythm.

The four groups worth checking every week

Late regularsTwo or more orders, now clearly past their usual gapStill warm, and the easiest to win back

How Loyalbase decides someone is late

For each customer with at least two orders, Loyalbase takes the median gap between their orders. That's the middle value, so one odd gap doesn't skew it. A customer counts as late when the days since their last order are more than the largest of these three numbers:

  • one and a half times their usual gap
  • their usual gap plus 21 days
  • 45 days

Yearly occasions are found when the same person orders within ten days of the same date in different years. Big public gifting dates such as Valentine's Day, Mother's Day and Christmas are left out, so everyone who orders for Christmas isn't treated as having a personal occasion.

Loyalbase also matches the same person across orders by phone or email, and ignores numbers shared by four or more different customers, like an office line.

Do it yourself in a spreadsheet

  1. Export your orders. In WooCommerce, go to Analytics, then Orders, then Download. In Shopify admin, export orders as a CSV file. Keep the customer email or phone, the order date and the order total.
  2. Sort by customer, then by date.
  3. For each customer, count their orders and note their last order date.
  4. Work out their usual gap. For customers with two or more orders, find the days between each pair of orders and take the middle value.
  5. Work out days since their last order: today minus the last order date.
  6. Flag them as late if days since their last order is more than all three: 1.5 times their usual gap, their usual gap plus 21, and 45.
  7. Filter first time buyers separately: one order, placed 45 to 150 days ago.

Expect this to take an hour or two the first time, and to be out of date a week later.

Questions

How long before a customer counts as lost?

In Loyalbase, a customer with no order in more than 12 months counts as not seen in a year. Those with two or more past orders also go on the stopped ordering list, because they're worth one honest message.

What is a good repeat purchase rate?

It depends heavily on what you sell. Stores selling things people run out of, like coffee, pet food or skincare, see far more repeat orders than stores selling furniture. Compare yourself with your own numbers from last year rather than a general benchmark.
