7 WhatsApp messages that bring customers back

Short, personal and sent at the right moment. Copy them, change the words to sound like you, and send from your shop's own WhatsApp.

Updated 4 November 20265 min readBy the Loyalbase team
Short answer

The best WhatsApp message to bring a customer back is short, personal and sent at the right moment: when a regular is later than usual, two weeks before a yearly occasion, 45 to 150 days after a first order, or a day or two after an unpaid checkout. Use their first name, sign with your shop's name, ask one easy question, and leave discounts for later.

Late regular

The customer who has gone quiet

When to send: a customer with two or more orders has gone clearly longer than their usual gap. For example, someone who orders every three weeks and hasn't ordered in six.

Hi [Name], it's been a little while! We'd love to have you back. Anything we can get ready for you this week? [Shop name]

Why it works: No discount and no guilt. It reminds them you exist and gives them an easy yes.

Yearly occasion

The birthday or anniversary they order for every year

When to send: about two weeks before the date they ordered for last year.

Hi [Name]! Your yearly [birthday] order is coming up on [date]. Shall we get something ready for you? [Shop name]

Why it works: You solve a problem before they remember they have it. It works best for flowers, cakes and gifts. If you know who the order is for, try: "[Recipient]'s birthday is coming up on [date]. Would you like us to get something ready again this year?"

Unpaid checkout

The checkout that stopped at payment

When to send: within a day or two of a checkout that didn't get paid.

Hi [Name], we noticed your order didn't go through. Can we help you finish it, or would you like to pay another way? [Shop name]

Why it works: Many unpaid checkouts are failed cards or payment worries, not lost interest. Offering another way to pay, like bank transfer or cash on delivery, removes the blocker.

First time buyer

The customer who bought once

When to send: 45 to 150 days after their first order, if they haven't ordered again.

Hi [Name], thank you again for your first order with [Shop name]! We hope you loved it. Anything we can get for you next?

Why it works: The second order is where a buyer becomes a customer. A thank you with an open question feels like service, not marketing.

Stopped ordering

The past regular you haven't seen in a year

When to send: when someone with two or more past orders hasn't bought in over a year.

Hi [Name], we've missed you at [Shop name]! A lot has changed since your last order. Can we tempt you this week?

Why it works: It's honest about the gap, and "a lot has changed" gives them a reason to look. If you have a new product, name it.

Best customer

Your favourite customers

When to send: any time, and especially before a busy season.

Hi [Name], thank you for being one of our favourite customers. It means a lot to us. [Shop name]

Why it works: There's no ask. Your best customers are the ones most likely to recommend you, and a thank you keeps them close.

Corporate buyer

The person who orders for their office

When to send: a few weeks before office events, year end and festive seasons.

Hi [Name], thank you for the orders for your team! Planning anything for the office soon? We can put together a box for any size. [Shop name]

Why it works: One office order can be worth many personal ones. A reminder before the season puts you first in line.

Seven rules for win back messages that don't annoy people

  1. Only message people who said yes. Meta requires businesses to get permission before messaging people on WhatsApp, and the permission has to name your business. A tick box at checkout is the simplest way to get it.
  2. Space them out. One message per person every two weeks at most is a good rule.
  3. Send in the day. Never late at night or early in the morning.
  4. Use their name and your shop's name. A message that could have come from anyone gets ignored.
  5. Ask one easy question. "Anything we can get ready for you this week?" is easier to answer than a link to your catalogue.
  6. Lead without a discount. Save offers for people who don't reply, so you don't teach customers to wait for a code.
  7. Always honour stop. If someone asks you to stop, stop, and note it so nobody on your team messages them again.

Send one by one, or in bulk?

For a handful of people a day, send from the WhatsApp Business app on your phone. It's personal and free.

Questions

Is it OK to message customers on WhatsApp?

Yes, if they gave you their number and agreed to hear from your business. Meta requires this permission for business messages, and data protection laws in many countries apply too. If someone asks you to stop, stop.

What's the best time to send?

Send during daytime hours when your customers would normally shop, and never late at night. Try two different times for a few weeks and keep the one that gets more replies.

Should I offer a discount to bring customers back?

Not in the first message. A personal check in works for many customers on its own. Keep a small offer for people who don't reply, so discounts don't become the reason anyone orders.

How many customers should I message a day?

Only as many as you can reply to properly. A fast, friendly reply is part of what brings them back.
